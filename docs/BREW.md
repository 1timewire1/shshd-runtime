# The BREW games

*Surviving High School* '07 and '08 ran on Qualcomm BREW phones, built by Centerscore. They share one engine and are a calendar simulation: a story schedules events on dates and times, and a day planner runs them. This fork is translating their stories into ordinary episodes.

**State: in progress.** The translator and the `shsbrew` addon exist. The whole '08 Campaign plays through (63 stories), and the first 18 Classic stories (Stage 3a, 2026-11-06), with their calendar. Seven stories were first: Quizzes 1-3, 2.2, 2.5, 3.3 and 9.4. Scripted runs of them match Tardy-Slip's lines, speakers and menus on eight answer paths each. Most other stories still stop on a service with no handler yet (the catch game, for one), which is deliberate: a story that needs something new says so instead of being guessed at.

None of the games' code runs here, and no file of them ships with the runtime: `tools/translate` reads the player's own copies.

## Building

```sh
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --base-08 GAME_08_FOLDER --base-07 MODULE_07_FOLDER --casts BREW_CASTS_JSON --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --story FILE --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --classic --story FILE --classic-folder CLASSIC_08_FOLDER --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --classic-copy --story CLASSIC_08_FILE --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
```

The first writes the addon, and the rest a story that reads it: a Campaign story, a Classic story (`'07`; `--classic-folder` is '08's `addons/Classic`, where an '07 file finds its number by title), or '08's `(Classic)` copy of one. `brew_casts.json` is the BREW portrait catalogue's list of each story's looks.

## How a story is translated

BREW's machine is SHS's, in KiWi's older header, so a story keeps its scripts, with every host call rewritten for SHS's services (`HANDLERS` in `brew.py`).

- **Scripts.** A story's 25001 stays where it is, and its story script, 26000, becomes 25002, since 26000 and up are SHS's episode bank.
- **Choices.** A quiz has no player character, so it asks in the narrator's gold under `Make your choice!`, as New Rules' Train That Brain! does.
- **Word games.** BREW's timed menus come from one shared routine: several rounds of a spinning cursor over words with scores, usually two good and two bad, returning a total the story tests. They play as service 71, with BREW's own title and instructions, and its result is scaled onto the best total, so that a full score is every round's best. A single round is a question, and it's the smartphone's `Think fast!`, which takes a random option when time runs out. Two menus have three words (Stage 5: Skating Dating one good and two bad, Artistic Differences two good and one bad), where native's word game needs two of each: a word game of three words, at least one of each, shows all three each round, shuffled (`WordGame.three_words`), in the BREW packs only (`TRANSLATED_THREE_WORD_PACKS`): elsewhere it stays the stop it is natively.
- **Menus** (28, 30): as BREW's, a blank last option, the part after a final `|`, isn't shown (`FUN_0000d6a0`; `engine.brew_menus`, the profile's `TRANSLATED_BREW_MENU_PACKS`). Fall '06's math questions build their answers ending in `|`. SHS's own menus keep the blank field, as the smartphone does. The decompiled BREW engine was checked against everything here on 2026-11-07 (reference/Other Games/docs/SHS_BREW_ENGINE.md §6.1).
- **Portrait choice** (27: count, list; Stage 4) is the smartphone's portrait picker (78), shown by the wait after it (74), whose
  answer is the chosen one's place in the list, as BREW's. Each photo's look is resolved first, as a speaker's is. BREW's title,
  `Pick Your Best Self Portrait`, is wider than the HD picker's one line, so it reads `Pick a Self Portrait`. The M.A.S.H. packs
  offer four photos or ten, each in the pack's own picker layout: four as New Rules' four faces (the native slots leave a side
  empty), ten with the chosen photo in front and nine smaller behind and beside it. Keys 1-9 and 0 pick photos one to ten. The frame and the picker's panel follow the highlighted photo, blue for a boy and pink for a girl (the native picker's
  panel keeps its first portrait's colour, which other episodes still do; `desktop_picker.panel_theme`).
- **"a(n)"**. BREW writes `a(n) %s` where a script fills in a word (M.A.S.H. Pack 1's houses and cars, Fall '06's grades). In
  the BREW packs (`TRANSLATED_ARTICLE_PACKS`) the format service (22) picks "a" or "an" by the word: by its first letter, with a
  few words said otherwise (`a university`, `an hour`), and a single letter by its name (`an A`, `a B`, `an F`;
  `engine.choose_articles`; owner, 2026-11-07).
- **The story script** is 26000, or 25002 in a story without one: the M.A.S.H. packs' setup schedules 25002.
- **Name entry** (29: title, default, 0) is the smartphone's own text input (17). Its answer becomes the event's text (`$BREWEVENT`), which BREW's scripts read straight after with 7 and store as `$USR`, `$CAT_NAME` or `$ANNA`.
- **The Java '08 Look** (a switch, off by default, for the '08 Campaign only; '07's stories never had it): the Java '08 demo's "SHS" tile backdrop with its name bar around the strip, its relationship icons and small pictures, and its catch game's skin. The add-on carries them when built with the demo's jar (`--java-08`, `brew_java.py`), 10000 above BREW's (profile `JAVA_LOOK_ART`).
- **Skipping.** The catch game's skip prompt offers each outcome its story has (`choice_hints.brew_catch_outcomes`, as the word games': about 30 of the Campaign's catch games also part at 30). M.A.S.H. 3 and 4 part every few points (the score joins the total that picks the fortune: 15 ways), so a prompt with more outcomes than seven rows hold goes in pages of five, each with Next Page (the last back to the first) and Play, in a panel a little taller than a story's choice may be (`Desktop._open_prompt`; owner, 2026-11-07). A turned page is there at once, with no closing, fade-in or fly-on, and the search narrows each change with previews that stop where the two stories part, which halves M.A.S.H. 3 and 4's (0.6 to 0.3 s on the desktop) and takes about a third off the rest, outcomes unchanged. The translator now finds cut-offs for nearly every Classic catch game too (`catch_cuts`): a score kept in the helper's own local is read only within that helper (as `0x5f 0x3f` or `0x41 n`, the constant first or second), and a score never read, or never taken before a service answering in R replaces it, changes nothing. 103 of the 105 catch games the BREW stories reach get them (only M.A.S.H. 3 and 4, which add the score to a total, need the full search), and every one matches the full search. On the g54 the prompt went from 1.2-1.9 s to about 0.25 s; M.A.S.H. 3 and 4 still take about 2 s. A word game's Win (32767) is a pass before BREW's routine scales anything.
- **The catch game.** BREW's one minigame, in three kinds (19 orbit, 20 angled, 21 sideways), plays as service 221 with "Use Translated Mini-Games" on, ported from the module's own code (`brew_catch.py`) and drawn with its art at 3 times (`desktop_brew_catch.py`). Its page shows first, as in BREW. The orbit moves once a tick, as in BREW, at the only real phone's 50 ms (its `xlarge.prf`): a lap takes 2 s. With the switch off it's a word game under the same page, less the catch game's points (`45 pts to pass.`) and with `Think fast!`, the smartphone's kind of banner, for BREW's generic title `Instructions` (`CATCH_POINTS`, `CATCH_GENERIC_TITLE`); BREW's own banners, such as `Bowl right!`, stay. A game won by the lowest score (9.2's two, `Get the lowest score you can!`, `CATCH_LOW`) swaps its lists and turns its result around. Two words for each picture, once each however many kinds share it (`CATCH_WORDS`, named by eye), good or bad by the picture's value. That result out of the smartphone's pass mark is that share of the target: every band the 181 call sites test can be reached that way.
- **6.1 Surviving College** picks its ending from 6 points up or below 5, so exactly 5 has none and the story stops, in BREW too. Here 5 takes the lower ending (owner's choice, `STORY_FIXES`).
- **Title line and long lines.** A story's first line, `Surviving High School` and its title, isn't shown: the title card has it. Lines over 190 characters, the smartphone's own longest, go out as several at sentence ends.
- **Chapter 1's captions** (`Wednesday, after school: Tryouts.`, 1.1 to 1.3 only) are time badges (90) for the next message, `WED - Tryouts`, with BREW's own icons from the add-on (revision 2, 26400 + a picture's ID).
- **The title card** has the event's day under the title, `Friday, November 9`, from the setup's own calendar (its events 33 and 69, run at translation). The BREW packs' cards show the date as the title and the story's name under it (profile `TRANSLATED_DATE_FIRST_PACKS`, `Desktop._date_first`; owner, 2026-11-07), as a story over several days opens each day with a card; an episode's preview picture keeps the story's name as the title. A Classic story's has the day its first event runs on, written by the driver as a date card's is: CS Idol's setup schedules its last school day last (Stage 5).
- **Quotes and brackets** (owner, 2026-11-06; `tidy`, `shown_parts`). '07's stories wrap every spoken line in double quotes, which SHS's box doesn't: a line, or each option of a menu, wrapped whole loses them. A choice option wrapped whole in brackets loses those too (`(Kiss Angie.)`); a line keeps its brackets, as they make it narration or a thought. A line made of quoted speech and bracketed asides splits when the speech comes first (`"Excellent." (She opens a pocket.)`: the line, then the aside as the speaker's thought), and stays whole without its quotes when an aside comes first (`(Whispers.) Calm down...`). Quotes inside a line and apostrophes stay, but speech that quotes a name or title inside it loses its outer pair (`I just got this one, "Alone," that's pretty cool.`; `nested_quotes`: the inner quotes must pair up, so narrated `"A" and "B"` stays). An option both quoted and bracketed loses both (Male Cheerleader's `"(Point over at Beth and say, "...")"`), and '07's name entry title `(Enter name:)` is `Enter name:` (owner, 2026-11-07). An option too long for two lines gets a taller row in the HD choice panel (`hd_choice`), not a shortened text. About 14,400 strings in '07, 19 in the '08 Campaign (18 of them word-game words, already shown bare).
- **Names in word games.** Service 71 shows its texts raw, as the native game does, so a word game's title and question get their `$NAME` tags filled in by the story (`named_texts`): `Don't worry, $USR!` reads `Don't worry, Matt!`.
- **Brackets.** BREW puts narration and thoughts alike in brackets. Narration loses them, and a character's bracketed line becomes SHS's thought. Game words lose their brackets and quotes.
- **Time.** Each Campaign story runs one calendar event, so scheduling it just queues its script. A Classic story (`build_classic`, pack 31100, by its Classic number; an '07 copy takes its '08 file's; the files without one follow 73 by name, `CLASSIC_UNNUMBERED`, and French Connection's ends its name) runs on a calendar kept in saved numbers, as Battle of the Schools keeps its own: its events (33) go on a list with their minute, length, icon, flag, day and 16 fields, and their text and name under keyed strings (`$BREWEV<n>`, `$BREWNAME<n>`). The story script's prologue is the driver (`calendar_step`): it closes the timed event that ran, picks the next by BREW's rule (Tardy-Slip's `brew-calendar.js`: in each half hour a flagged event first, an untimed one when its slot comes; past the day's end, the next of Monday, Wednesday and Friday, up to the calendar's last day), queues itself behind it and runs it, with the event's fields as the registers 8 reads and its text as 7's. The first event comes under the title card; a new date gets a card of its own (`Wednesday, April 12`); each later event shows a badge with its name and BREW icon, or the smartphone's location badge (3104) when it has none, and an event with no name (CS Idol's lunchtimes) has no badge, as BREW's planner has nothing to show for it. A badge goes after its first message in any of a story's scripts (its flag and icon are saved numbers). Each badge shows the time the planner jumps to, the event's half-hour slot, then its name: `3:00 PM - Practice` (`push_badge_time`; owner, 2026-11-07). A school day's caption, `School
March %d` filled in with today's date (CS Idol), is said in BREW; here it's that event's badge, `9:00 AM - School`, with its icon. An event with no name (CS Idol's lunchtimes, every event of The School Paper) shows just its time, `12:00 PM`, under the location badge, unless a badge that no message has shown yet is still up (CS Idol's school badge, which comes with lunch's first line); BREW's planner has no row for them, so these are for the owner's badge review. Dates are on the cards, not the badges. The first line said after an event starts (not the title card's), its opener (`Lunch time.`, `After school.`), ends in three dots instead of one when it's short narration ending in a single stop, badge or not, so it runs on into what follows (`dots`); so does the line said with one of Chapter 1's caption badges. An event whose icon is a story's own picture the add-on carries (CS Idol's Practice: the yawning face, 26501) shows that picture (revision 7, `OWN_ICON_BASE`), as BREW's planner does; any other own picture takes the location badge.
- **Several dates (Stage 5).** An event's script may be a script of the story's own besides the story script (CS Idol's school days, 26001): those follow 25002 (`script_map`), the event keeps its script, and the driver queues it ahead of itself; scripts nothing schedules or names go. Weekly events (34: weekdays as `Mo,We,Fr`, from one date to another) are a copy on the list for each of those days, and a field set on the first (35) reaches every copy. The weekdays are a constant the translator reads (a string in the script's data). 42 and 43 give the next school day's date and today's (`today_date`). A speaker's name plate (15) lasts one line only.
- **Fall '06** (`build_season`, pack 31200, Stage 6): '07's built-in term runs on the season driver (`season_step`), a script of its own (25099) holding a port of `brew-calendar.js`: each event kept once and a list of events per day, as BREW keeps its `events` and `entries` (a weekly class is one event on every school day), the due list, and the clock's `checked` mark. It adds the services only the season uses (36 to 48 but 40 and 42/43, which the Classic driver has; 56/57 flags; 66; 67). Its scene stack has room of its own, as the season opens more scenes than it closes. Its dates (owner, 2026-11-07): a card each week, `Week 1 of 13` over the dates of the week's school days (`September 13-17`, `September 27 - October 1`), as Football Star's week cards, the first one included; a narration line opening each new day (`Monday, September 13.`); and the time badges of the Classic stories. Its cards keep the title on top (it isn't one of the date-first packs). Events BREW runs inside another (37 lists them) run at the script's next wait (72, 12, 74 or 29), or as the driver next starts: the nest runner (25098) runs each to its end through runtime service 223, which runs a script and comes back to its caller (saved, version 39). As in the native engine (`FUN_0000af08`), they run one after another, never one inside another; Tardy-Slip nests them, so its runner is guarded for the comparison.
- **`(Classic)` copies** (`build_classic_copy`, Stage 3b). Funky Monkey is the one story with two entries (owner, 2026-11-07): '08 rewrote its text, so its '08 file is an entry of its own under `'08` (the Campaign's pack at 9200 plus its Classic number, 9238, after the specials), titled `(Classic)`, on the calendar, with '08's shared locations and its own pictures as '08 shows them. Each keeps its own writing; '07's takes '08's fixes to its code (`CONTEXT_FIXES`): five endings open on its own scene (14 to 26505), where '07's kept whatever was showing. Its date, a day later in '07 and still a Monday, stays '07's. Blood Drive and M.A.S.H. Pack 1 are one `'07` entry each, built from '08's file, which is '07's story with '08's fixes: Blood Drive's perfect-score ending, which '07 never reaches (two kind choices now score, and 13 points or more wins), a scene change before the concert and one word; M.A.S.H. Pack 1's `Hero Firefighter` and a floor on one relationship. The 13 other stories '08 reissued are the same in both files but for the list title.
- **Pages and pauses.** BREW's page (12) draws and returns, and the script waits for a key with a PAUSE (0x32). SHS's page, or the game screen it becomes, already waits for a tap, so a pause straight after a page goes (`drop_page_pauses`); left in, it was a stop with nothing on screen. A page before a catch game still moves onto the game's screen with a music call between (Trick or Treat).
- **Name plate** (15, one story's helper): the next line goes out under that name with no portrait (76).
- **Numbers.** BREW's numbered values work as SHS's do, except 407: BREW's runs 0 to 100 and SHS's counts icons, so BREW's value stays under its own key and 407 follows it. Below 20, 30 and 40 show 3, 2 and 1 skulls. From 40, 66 and 76 show 1, 2 and 3 smileys, or hearts for the current partner.
- **Portraits.** A look is a 23-entry list that scripts build and change. Its entries live in saved numbers with a running hash. When a character is next shown, a changed hash is looked up in the story's table of looks, and the character is pointed at that look's faces. Setups are still big, as a story can dress 27 characters: `Session.advance`'s safety limit went from 100,000 instructions to a million for it. Entry 2 picks the box's colour, as BREW's pink one does, and a look that isn't a person's (entry 0 no picture, or the computer, phone or TV: `GREY_BODIES`) takes the smartphone's grey box, as its things speak (owner, 2026-11-07). Animals keep a person's.
- **Scenes.** Scenes stack: 13 opens one and 18 closes the top one, so what's underneath comes back. The stack lives in saved numbers.
- **The list.** The `'07` group comes before `'08`'s (LEGACY_GAMES `section`), though its pack is the higher.
- **Names.** BREW's stories have their own names tables (`TRANSLATED_NAME_SETS`), as its phones' `prefs.sav` was its own, and '07 and '08 each have one (owner, 2026-11-06), as they were separate apps. '08's stories set `$USR` to Jenny when it's empty; '07 has no default, and its name comes from Fall '06's name entry (49) or the 20 Classic stories that ask (29). `$USR` is the only tag the games share between stories: the rest (M.A.S.H.'s `$MASH_*`, `$CAT_NAME`, `$ANNA`...) stay within a story or a series.
- **Narration.** A character named Event is the narrator, as on the smartphone. The real game shows it with an `EVENT` plate and an empty photo frame.
- **Title cards.** A story's card shows the first picture the story itself does, found by playing it that far when translating, as scripts choose pictures through helpers.

## Art and music

All of it is in the addon, and stories hold only their scripts ([ADDONS.md](ADDONS.md#shsbrew-the-brew-games)).

- **Locations** are 240 × 80 strips over a sky drawn in code, so each is rendered at each of its three skies, at six times size with no smoothing. '07-badged stories show '07's copies of the 28 shared locations (decision 7B); **'08 Backgrounds** (a switch, off by default) shows '08's instead (profile `BREW_08_BACKGROUNDS`). Every other graphic a story uses is the same in both games' base packs (checked 2026-11-06); only the publisher splash (8) and the title screen (47) differ, and neither is used.
- **King of the Road's Michael** is a whole picture of the story's own (26001) with eyes and mouth over it, anchored 4.5 art pixels left of its middle, so BREW draws him off-centre. From revision 7 a look whose body is a story's own picture is centred on it when it's 2 or more off (`CENTRE_OWN_BODY`; owner, 2026-11-07). The four other own-picture looks are centred already.
- **A Classic story's own catch picture.** Invisible Man and Skating Dating catch a yawning face that isn't in the base pack: their own 26501 (CS Idol uses it as an event icon). It's the same picture in all three, so from revision 6 the add-on carries it with the catch game's pictures under its own number (`OWN_CATCH_PICTURES`, by SHA-1); a story whose 26501 is something else gets no size for it, and its catch game stops as before.
- **A Classic story's own backgrounds** (97 in 56 stories, 12 different pictures, shipped in each story's pack) are in the add-on from revision 4, at 29000 up, with each story's table in the spec (`backgrounds`). '08 redrew 11 of the 12 as its extra locations (2000-2011): the theme park, plaza, shop street, suburb street, science lab, cafeteria, gazebo park, nurse's office, lake, restaurant and parking lot, but not the night skyline. From revision 5 the add-on carries those redraws 500 above the pictures they replace (`OWN_08_REDRAWS`, by fingerprint), and '08 Backgrounds swaps them too (owner, 2026-11-06). Contact sheets: `reference/screenshots/verify/brew_07_08/`.
- **Portraits** are paper dolls, rendered at 3.5 times size to 252 × 294, within twice the player's portrait mask. Anything larger is squeezed into the mask's shape by the player, out of proportion. They're made at seven times with no smoothing, then halved, so every art pixel is the same size. Each is raised 7 art pixels from the picture's bottom, which the ring hides, so the whole bust shows.
- **Relationship icons** are BREW's own, on the smartphone's places and motion (`TRANSLATED_BADGE_ART`). BREW's never break, so a broken one is the whole one parted down its middle.
- **The frame** around the strip is BREW's dialogue panel, tiled at the strip's density, with its yellow bar above and below (`TRANSLATED_FRAME_BACKDROP`). As in the real game, the panel turns pink behind a pink box and back for anyone else (`TRANSLATED_THEME_BACKDROP`).
- **Music** is rendered through Java's built-in synthesizer, as for New Rules and '11. The game plays its 15 cues all the same way; sorted by ear, 8001, 8004, 8008, 8009, 8011 and 8012 play as sound effects, so they leave any music playing, and the rest as music. 8011 and 8001 are the correct and incorrect chimes, which Answer hints read as outcomes (`shs/profile.py` `TRANSLATED_FEEDBACK_MUSIC`).

## Checking

The BREW games' behaviour comes from a local copy of the third-party Tardy-Slip player, checked against the native modules. The real game also runs under the Melange wrapper on an Android emulator, which can settle how something looks, but it's silent. A translated story should say the same lines in the same order on the same choices. Where a story picks at random (BREW's 6), both sides must take the same picks: the Tardy-Slip runner can write every draw it takes (`RANDOM_LOG`), and the comparison answers each of the translation's random calls with the next one, scaled as Tardy-Slip scales it (`|floor(r x 0x7fffffff)| mod bound`). With that, Trick or Treat, King of the Road, 9.1 and the M.A.S.H. packs match on every path. The local copy has a debug hook (2026-11-07): `globalThis.tardy` holds the running story's front and story, so `tardy.front.brewPlanner({ day, from, to })` shows the planner for any day (`tardy.story.brew` is the calendar). `play-tardy-slip.ps1` in the SHSHD folder opens it. Tardy-Slip's runner also draws inside its word games and catch games, which a translation's skipped ones don't, so a story that picks at random after one (The School Paper's quiz shuffles) matches once the replay starts that many draws in (25 there). The calendar's days are checked too: the runner's `calendarEvent` gives each event's date, and every Stage 5 story's date cards are those days.
