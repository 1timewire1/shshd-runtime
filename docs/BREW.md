# The BREW games

*Surviving High School* '07 and '08 ran on Qualcomm BREW phones, built by Centerscore. They share one engine and are a calendar simulation: a story schedules events on dates and times, and a day planner runs them. This fork is translating their stories into ordinary episodes.

**State: in progress.** The translator and the `shsbrew` addon exist. The whole '08 Campaign plays through (63 stories), and the first 18 Classic stories (Stage 3a, 2026-11-06), with their calendar. Seven stories were first: Quizzes 1-3, 2.2, 2.5, 3.3 and 9.4. Scripted runs of them match Tardy-Slip's lines, speakers and menus on eight answer paths each. Most other stories still stop on a service with no handler yet (the catch game, for one), which is deliberate: a story that needs something new says so instead of being guessed at.

None of the games' code runs here, and no file of them ships with the runtime: `tools/translate` reads the player's own copies.

## Building

```sh
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --base-08 GAME_08_FOLDER --base-07 MODULE_07_FOLDER --casts BREW_CASTS_JSON --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --story FILE --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --classic --story FILE --classic-folder CLASSIC_08_FOLDER --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
python tools/translate/translate.py brew --classic-copy --story CLASSIC_08_FILE --addon shsbrew.exp --out OUT_DIR
```

The first writes the addon, and the rest a story that reads it: a Campaign story, a Classic story (`'07`; `--classic-folder` is '08's `addons/Classic`, where an '07 file finds its number by title), or '08's `(Classic)` copy of one. `brew_casts.json` is the BREW portrait catalogue's list of each story's looks.

## How a story is translated

BREW's machine is SHS's, in KiWi's older header, so a story keeps its scripts, with every host call rewritten for SHS's services (`HANDLERS` in `brew.py`).

- **Scripts.** A story's 25001 stays where it is, and its story script, 26000, becomes 25002, since 26000 and up are SHS's episode bank.
- **Choices.** A quiz has no player character, so it asks in the narrator's gold under `Make your choice!`, as New Rules' Train That Brain! does.
- **Word games.** BREW's timed menus come from one shared routine: several rounds of a spinning cursor over words with scores, usually two good and two bad, returning a total the story tests. They play as service 71, with BREW's own title and instructions, and its result is scaled onto the best total, so that a full score is every round's best. A single round is a question, and it's the smartphone's `Think fast!`, which takes a random option when time runs out.
- **Name entry** (29: title, default, 0) is the smartphone's own text input (17). Its answer becomes the event's text (`$BREWEVENT`), which BREW's scripts read straight after with 7 and store as `$USR`, `$CAT_NAME` or `$ANNA`.
- **The Java '08 Look** (a switch, off by default, for the '08 Campaign only; '07's stories never had it): the Java '08 demo's "SHS" tile backdrop with its name bar around the strip, its relationship icons and small pictures, and its catch game's skin. The add-on carries them when built with the demo's jar (`--java-08`, `brew_java.py`), 10000 above BREW's (profile `JAVA_LOOK_ART`).
- **Skipping.** The catch game's skip prompt offers each outcome its story has (`choice_hints.brew_catch_outcomes`, as the word games': about 30 of the Campaign's catch games also part at 30). A word game's Win (32767) is a pass before BREW's routine scales anything.
- **The catch game.** BREW's one minigame, in three kinds (19 orbit, 20 angled, 21 sideways), plays as service 221 with "Use Translated Mini-Games" on, ported from the module's own code (`brew_catch.py`) and drawn with its art at 3 times (`desktop_brew_catch.py`). Its page shows first, as in BREW. The orbit moves once a tick, as in BREW, at the only real phone's 50 ms (its `xlarge.prf`): a lap takes 2 s. With the switch off it's a word game under the same page, less the catch game's points (`45 pts to pass.`) and with `Think fast!`, the smartphone's kind of banner, for BREW's generic title `Instructions` (`CATCH_POINTS`, `CATCH_GENERIC_TITLE`); BREW's own banners, such as `Bowl right!`, stay. A game won by the lowest score (9.2's two, `Get the lowest score you can!`, `CATCH_LOW`) swaps its lists and turns its result around. Two words for each picture, once each however many kinds share it (`CATCH_WORDS`, named by eye), good or bad by the picture's value. That result out of the smartphone's pass mark is that share of the target: every band the 181 call sites test can be reached that way.
- **6.1 Surviving College** picks its ending from 6 points up or below 5, so exactly 5 has none and the story stops, in BREW too. Here 5 takes the lower ending (owner's choice, `STORY_FIXES`).
- **Title line and long lines.** A story's first line, `Surviving High School` and its title, isn't shown: the title card has it. Lines over 190 characters, the smartphone's own longest, go out as several at sentence ends.
- **Chapter 1's captions** (`Wednesday, after school: Tryouts.`, 1.1 to 1.3 only) are time badges (90) for the next message, `WED - Tryouts`, with BREW's own icons from the add-on (revision 2, 26400 + a picture's ID).
- **The title card** has the event's day under the title, `Friday, November 9`, from the setup's own calendar (its events 33 and 69, run at translation).
- **Quotes and brackets** (owner, 2026-11-06; `tidy`, `shown_parts`). '07's stories wrap every spoken line in double quotes, which SHS's box doesn't: a line, or each option of a menu, wrapped whole loses them. A choice option wrapped whole in brackets loses those too (`(Kiss Angie.)`); a line keeps its brackets, as they make it narration or a thought. A line made of quoted speech and bracketed asides splits when the speech comes first (`"Excellent." (She opens a pocket.)`: the line, then the aside as the speaker's thought), and stays whole without its quotes when an aside comes first (`(Whispers.) Calm down...`). Quotes inside a line and apostrophes stay, but speech that quotes a name or title inside it loses its outer pair (`I just got this one, "Alone," that's pretty cool.`; `nested_quotes`: the inner quotes must pair up, so narrated `"A" and "B"` stays). An option both quoted and bracketed loses both (Male Cheerleader's `"(Point over at Beth and say, "...")"`), and '07's name entry title `(Enter name:)` is `Enter name:` (owner, 2026-11-07). An option too long for two lines gets a taller row in the HD choice panel (`hd_choice`), not a shortened text. About 14,400 strings in '07, 19 in the '08 Campaign (18 of them word-game words, already shown bare).
- **Names in word games.** Service 71 shows its texts raw, as the native game does, so a word game's title and question get their `$NAME` tags filled in by the story (`named_texts`): `Don't worry, $USR!` reads `Don't worry, Matt!`.
- **Brackets.** BREW puts narration and thoughts alike in brackets. Narration loses them, and a character's bracketed line becomes SHS's thought. Game words lose their brackets and quotes.
- **Time.** Each Campaign story runs one calendar event, so scheduling it just queues its script. A Classic story (`build_classic`, pack 31100, by its Classic number; an '07 copy takes its '08 file's; the files without one follow 73 by name, `CLASSIC_UNNUMBERED`, and French Connection's ends its name) runs on a calendar kept in saved numbers, as Battle of the Schools keeps its own: its events (33) go on a list with their minute, length, icon, flag, day and 16 fields, and their text and name under keyed strings (`$BREWEV<n>`, `$BREWNAME<n>`). The story script's prologue is the driver (`calendar_step`): it closes the timed event that ran, picks the next by BREW's rule (Tardy-Slip's `brew-calendar.js`: in each half hour a flagged event first, an untimed one when its slot comes; past the day's end, the next of Monday, Wednesday and Friday, up to the calendar's last day), queues itself behind it and runs it, with the event's fields as the registers 8 reads and its text as 7's. The first event comes under the title card; a new date gets a card of its own (`Wednesday, April 12`); each later event shows a badge with its name and BREW icon, or the smartphone's location badge (3104) when it has none. Events inside events (Fall '06) aren't built yet.
- **`(Classic)` copies** (`build_classic_copy`, Stage 3b). Where '08's file of a Classic story really differs from '07's (Funky Monkey; Blood Drive and M.A.S.H. Pack 1 at their stages), it's an entry of its own under `'08`: the Campaign's pack at 9200 plus its Classic number (Funky Monkey is 9238, after the specials), titled `(Classic)`, on the calendar, with '08's shared locations and its own pictures as '08 shows them.
- **Pages and pauses.** BREW's page (12) draws and returns, and the script waits for a key with a PAUSE (0x32). SHS's page, or the game screen it becomes, already waits for a tap, so a pause straight after a page goes (`drop_page_pauses`); left in, it was a stop with nothing on screen. A page before a catch game still moves onto the game's screen with a music call between (Trick or Treat).
- **Name plate** (15, one story's helper): the next line goes out under that name with no portrait (76).
- **Numbers.** BREW's numbered values work as SHS's do, except 407: BREW's runs 0 to 100 and SHS's counts icons, so BREW's value stays under its own key and 407 follows it. Below 20, 30 and 40 show 3, 2 and 1 skulls. From 40, 66 and 76 show 1, 2 and 3 smileys, or hearts for the current partner.
- **Portraits.** A look is a 23-entry list that scripts build and change. Its entries live in saved numbers with a running hash. When a character is next shown, a changed hash is looked up in the story's table of looks, and the character is pointed at that look's faces. Setups are still big, as a story can dress 27 characters: `Session.advance`'s safety limit went from 100,000 instructions to a million for it. Entry 2 picks the box's colour, as BREW's pink one does, and a look that isn't a person's (entry 0 no picture, or the computer, phone or TV: `GREY_BODIES`) takes the smartphone's grey box, as its things speak (owner, 2026-11-07). Animals keep a person's.
- **Scenes.** Scenes stack: 13 opens one and 18 closes the top one, so what's underneath comes back. The stack lives in saved numbers.
- **The list.** The `'07` group comes before `'08`'s (LEGACY_GAMES `section`), though its pack is the higher.
- **Names.** BREW's stories have their own names tables (`TRANSLATED_NAME_SETS`), as its phones' `prefs.sav` was its own, and '07 and '08 each have one (owner, 2026-11-06), as they were separate apps. '08's stories set `$USR` to Jenny when it's empty; '07 has no default, and its name comes from Fall '06's name entry (49) or the 20 Classic stories that ask (29). `$USR` is the only tag the games share between stories: the rest (M.A.S.H.'s `$MASH_*`, `$CAT_NAME`, `$ANNA`...) stay within a story or a series.
- **Narration.** A character named Event is the narrator, as on the smartphone. The real game shows it with an `EVENT` plate and an empty photo frame.
- **Title cards.** A story's card shows the first picture the story itself does, found by playing it that far when translating, as scripts choose pictures through helpers.

## Art and music

All of it is in the addon, and stories hold only their scripts ([ADDONS.md](ADDONS.md#shsbrew-the-brew-games)).

- **Locations** are 240 × 80 strips over a sky drawn in code, so each is rendered at each of its three skies, at six times size with no smoothing. '07-badged stories show '07's copies of the 28 shared locations (decision 7B); **'08 Backgrounds** (a switch, off by default) shows '08's instead (profile `BREW_08_BACKGROUNDS`). Every other graphic a story uses is the same in both games' base packs (checked 2026-11-06); only the publisher splash (8) and the title screen (47) differ, and neither is used.
- **A Classic story's own backgrounds** (97 in 56 stories, 12 different pictures, shipped in each story's pack) are in the add-on from revision 4, at 29000 up, with each story's table in the spec (`backgrounds`). '08 redrew 11 of the 12 as its extra locations (2000-2011): the theme park, plaza, shop street, suburb street, science lab, cafeteria, gazebo park, nurse's office, lake, restaurant and parking lot, but not the night skyline. From revision 5 the add-on carries those redraws 500 above the pictures they replace (`OWN_08_REDRAWS`, by fingerprint), and '08 Backgrounds swaps them too (owner, 2026-11-06). Contact sheets: `reference/screenshots/verify/brew_07_08/`.
- **Portraits** are paper dolls, rendered at 3.5 times size to 252 × 294, within twice the player's portrait mask. Anything larger is squeezed into the mask's shape by the player, out of proportion. They're made at seven times with no smoothing, then halved, so every art pixel is the same size. Each is raised 7 art pixels from the picture's bottom, which the ring hides, so the whole bust shows.
- **Relationship icons** are BREW's own, on the smartphone's places and motion (`TRANSLATED_BADGE_ART`). BREW's never break, so a broken one is the whole one parted down its middle.
- **The frame** around the strip is BREW's dialogue panel, tiled at the strip's density, with its yellow bar above and below (`TRANSLATED_FRAME_BACKDROP`). As in the real game, the panel turns pink behind a pink box and back for anyone else (`TRANSLATED_THEME_BACKDROP`).
- **Music** is rendered through Java's built-in synthesizer, as for New Rules and '11. The game plays its 15 cues all the same way; sorted by ear, 8001, 8004, 8008, 8009, 8011 and 8012 play as sound effects, so they leave any music playing, and the rest as music. 8011 and 8001 are the correct and incorrect chimes, which Answer hints read as outcomes (`shs/profile.py` `TRANSLATED_FEEDBACK_MUSIC`).

## Checking

The BREW games' behaviour comes from a local copy of the third-party Tardy-Slip player, checked against the native modules. The real game also runs under the Melange wrapper on an Android emulator, which can settle how something looks, but it's silent. A translated story should say the same lines in the same order on the same choices.
